Key Information

Environmental Impacts

  • New York City creates over 36 million pounds of plastic waste, much of it littering streets, parks, and waterways.
  • This includes approximately 742 million single use plastic bottles and an estimated 23 billion single use plastic bags every year
  • A significant portion of NYC’s plastic waste is burned at waste-to-energy incinerators. Incinerating plastics releases toxic pollutants and greenhouse gases, which degrades regional air quality.
  • Plastic takes centuries to fully break down. In NYC’s rivers, bays, and the Atlantic Ocean it degrades into microplastics ingested by marine life directly entering the local food chain.

Health Impacts

  • People consume hundreds of thousands of microplastics annually which are linked to increased rates of strokes, heart attacks and premature deaths.
  • A study by the New York City Environmental Justice Alliance found that neighborhoods with higher waste had increased rates of asthma and other respiratory illnesses.
  • The existence of plastic waste on the streets also causes pest infestations which can also spread diseases

Economic Impacts

  • According to the New York City Department of Sanitation (DSNY), the city spends millions dealing with plastic pollution.
  • Wealthier districts such as Manhattan’s Upper East Side experience higher sanitation and waste removal funding, which showcases the uneven distribution of services among the communities. There is a need for waste management to address the disparities and to ensure that all neighborhoods receive fair access to city services.
  • The inequities not only affect the city’s aesthetic but also perpetuates economic and environmental injustice. For a healthier city, we must prioritize resource distribution and invest in sustainable waste management solutions for the communities that don’t have it.
